3-STROKE ADDRESSING MECHANISM

	(see the mechanism emulator code vm1.c, vm1.h)

	MECHANISM

	The mechanism is a space consisting of addressable positions. The values contained in the space can be copied simultaneously.

	The space is divided into three parts: control, register and channels. The control part contains instructions that control copying. Channels are areas of space and instructions that point to them (this will be described and done in more detail in demo 3). The rest of the space (register) can be used to store data involved in calculations performed in the space.

	The mechanism does not use any arithmetic. Position indices are not numbers, but bit strings on which the only action (except copying) is performed - lexicographic comparison ">". The mechanism also has bit comparators "== 0", "== 1" for working with position attributes.

	The mechanism emulator code uses cycles that should be understood as the simultaneous execution of actions specified in the cycle.

	GENERATION OF MECHANISM

	The network of connections of the positions of space is initialized. Each position of space is assigned its unique index. The loader instruction is placed in the control area.

	The network of connections is defined by the sorting network. In demo 2 the odd even merge sort of the batcher is used (see sort.c).



ADDRESSING

	The idea of addressing is that instructions specifying copies are transformed into indexes that limit, but are not included in the copied intervals. In the first cycle, the positions corresponding to the indexes are found and the pairs of intervals are marked so that each interval of the pair is marked with the index of the conjugate interval. In the second cycle, all positions are sorted, and the intervals of the pairs are exchanged with simultaneous copying of the data corresponding to them. In the third cycle, the copied intervals are ordered, and along with them, the corresponding copied data are also ordered.

	ADDRESSING CONSISTENCY

	For correct operation, the mechanism requires that the lines addressed by the same tact instructions do not intersect. The alignment of lines is supposed to be done programmatically (this will be discussed in more detail in demo 4).



2-STROKE ADDRESSING MECHANISM

	A 2-stroke mechanism can be built with a stable sorting network.

	It consists of the first two cycles of the 3-cycle mechanism, and from the second cycle it is possible to remove the extra assignments concerning the "mark" index, which can also be removed.



ANNEX 1

ADDRESSING SPEED ASSESSMENT

	The speed of the comparator of 2 16-bit values with sequential comparison of bit values will be 16 clock cycles. However, if parallel comparison is used (with slightly higher transistor consumption), then logarithmic time consumption can be achieved. i.e. 4 clock cycles will be required to compare 16-bit values.

	The space emulation algorithm offers a 3-cycle mechanism. Each such cycle consists of layers of comparisons and copies. The space of size 2^16 positions has 135 layers.

	When executing the first clock cycle, the layer requires 2 consecutive uses of 4 comparators - two comparators with each associated position transposition, i.e. each of the 135 layers spends 2 * 4 = 8 parallel hardware ticks, plus 1-2 ticks for attribute checks. Thus, the first clock cycle spends approximately 1350 ticks.

	The second tick makes only one index comparison per layer and, together with the cost of processing position attributes, spends about 810 ticks.

	The third beat takes about the same amount of time as the first.

	In addition to the costs of executing the 3 main clock cycles of the addressing mechanism, there are also costs for preparing the clock cycles. However, they are relatively small, do not depend on the size of the space, and are executed in a few ticks. Thus, neglecting insignificant costs, one clock cycle of parallel addressing consumes approximately 3,510 hardware ticks.

	COMPARISON OF COMPUTING SYSTEM PERFORMANCE

	Since any computation can be reduced to addressable copies, it is proposed to measure the computation speed as the addressing speed, and the addressing speed to be measured in bits per second (or, alternatively, in the number of copies per second).



ANNEX 2
ESTIMATION OF TRANSISTOR AND CONNECTION COSTS
(for 2^16-position space)

	DATA

	6 transistors are spent on representing 1 bit. i.e. 576 transistors will be spent on representing the indices of one position, plus 2*6 on representing its attributes and 1*6 on storing the value. In total, 594 transistors are required to represent the data of one position of the subspace. In addition, 6 transistors will be required to represent the value located in the space, which together with the costs of the subspace gives 600 transistors.

	MECHANISMS

	A 1-bit ">" function can be created from 8 transistors. In order to make all the comparisons necessary in both positions of the transposition, it will be necessary to have 2 comparators in each position. Thus, the costs of each position for index comparison mechanisms will be 2 * 8 * 16 = 256 transistors. Bit attribute checking mechanisms will also be required - 2 * 8 = 16 transistors.

	In addition to the above, mechanisms for link sorting will also be needed. These mechanisms will consume not only transistors (no quantitative estimate is given), but also connections for transferring indexes, attributes and data - at least (16 + 2 + 1) * 135 = 2.565 connections.

	Thus, according to preliminary estimates, 3-cycle emulation of the entire space will require 39,321,600 transistors and 168,099,840 connections.



NOTES

	Quantitative estimates of the operating speed and consumption of the transistors of the parallel addressing mechanism are made on the basis of data provided by Wikipedia and other information resources. The author is not an expert in the field of circuit design.

	The parallel addressing algorithm can be improved. Its speed can be increased by 1.625 times by replacing the sorting network with a stable one. The speed can be further increased by reducing the number of network layers. It may also be possible to improve the algorithm so that it uses fewer indices, attributes, and actions.

	The proposed addressing scheme has a drawback: it does not allow addressing the extreme positions of space that are multiples of a power of two (base). There are two of these positions. The limitation is circumvented by a corresponding reduction in space for the last position (in the emulator, 2^16 - 16 is used instead of 2^16), and by placing the control region of space in lower addresses, which allows the zero address to be obscured by a bootloader instruction that is initialized before the space begins to work.

	The echo instruction loses the first character of the I/O channel. In demo 3 the channel service system will be replaced.
